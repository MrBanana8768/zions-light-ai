# Incident report — the 2026-08-24 conversation-chain incident

**Status:** resolved 2026-08-24. Corrective actions tracked in §8.
**Severity:** high — total loss of conversational continuity for the only
production user, undetected for ~10 hours. **No user data was lost**; see §2 for
the measurement that establishes this and for the earlier claim it corrects.
**Affected:** one production conversation (`<user-conv>`), one user.
**Repo state at time of writing:** `c6a16eb`, branch `docs/frontend-spec`.

> **On evidence.** This report separates what is **established** (verifiable in
> the repo, at a commit hash or `file:line`) from what is **inferred** (consistent
> with the record but not proven) and what is **testimony** (the engineer's
> account, uncorroborated because the relevant logs were never committed). Those
> labels are used throughout and are load-bearing. Several claims made during the

## 1. Summary

Between roughly 22:30 and 23:15 MST on 2026-08-23, during a window in which five
patch releases were cut and deployed in 87 minutes, a production conversation in
OpenWebUI acquired a corrupted message tree: 241 messages across **five roots**,
with the conversation pointer `currentId` sitting on an **8-message side branch**
rather than on the tip of the 208-message main chain.

Two harms followed from that single condition:

- **H1 — perceived loss.** OpenWebUI renders the thread by walking `parentId`
  back from `currentId`. The user saw an 8-message conversation where a
  208-message one had been. Her report: *"I just lost the complete chat history
  that I had."*
- **H2 — silent context truncation.** OpenWebUI builds its API payload by
  walking the *same* pointer. For roughly ten hours the compactor received **7 of
  241 messages** per request, with no error, no warning, and no log line stating
  that anything was short. The assistant behaved as if it had forgotten the
  relationship, which to a non-technical user is indistinguishable from ordinary
  model behaviour.

H1 announces itself. H2 does not, and H2 is the more serious harm. Both have the
same proximate cause: **one unvalidated pointer serving both the render path and
the send path, with nothing anywhere in the system holding an expected message
count to compare against.**

The condition was detected at approximately 09:02 MST on 2026-08-24, when the
user asked the assistant what it remembered and its answer was inconsistent with
what the memory store demonstrably contained. Direct measurement then showed
`chat_message` holding 241 rows for the chat while the compactor logged `msgs=7`
two seconds later.

Four repair attempts on the message tree either measured the wrong property or
were silently reverted. Resolution came from **abandoning repair**: the user's
compactor memory — 105 facts, 98 indexed episodic exchanges, 5 L1 summary chunks,
`last_summarized_turn=100` — had never been damaged, because it is keyed by
`conv_id` and lives outside OpenWebUI entirely. It was exported from
`<user-conv>` and imported into a fresh conversation `<user-conv-2>`
via the pre-existing `/admin/conversations/{id}/export` and
`/admin/conversations/import` endpoints, and chat ownership was transferred by
updating `chat.user_id` in `webui.db`. That fix held.

**Approximate accounting** (see §3 for the timestamp correction that produces
these numbers):

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Tree damage | ~45 min (22:30–23:15, 8/23), coincident with the release window |
| Damage → detection | ~10 h, the large majority overnight and unattended |
| Detection → resolution | ~3 h, including four failed repairs and the pivot |

## 2. Impact

### What the user experienced

1. A conversation of 208 messages rendered as a thread of 8.
2. For the following ten hours, an assistant that answered from 7 messages of
   context and behaved as though it did not know her.
3. On being asked directly what it remembered, an accurate but alarming answer:
   that it has no persistent memory across conversations. **That answer was
   correct.** All compactor memory is `conv_id`-scoped (`memory.py:137`), and the
   model was answering about cross-conversation memory, which it does not have.
   An early claim that the client had "made the model bear false witness" was
   raised and withdrawn on review; it is recorded here as withdrawn.
4. Resolution as a *new* conversation carrying all of her accumulated memory,
   rather than as a restored old one. The old transcript still exists in
   `webui.db` and in an off-pod tarball; it is not what she now uses.

### What was NOT lost

- **No message was ever deleted from OpenWebUI.** All 241 messages were present
  in both `chat.chat` (the JSON blob) and the `chat_message` table for the whole
  incident, confirmed by direct measurement: `BLOB total=241` / `TABLE total=241`.
  A pointer moved; nothing was erased.
- **No compactor memory was lost to the tree damage.** Facts, episodic index,
  summaries and persona are keyed by `conv_id`, which is derived independently of
  OpenWebUI's tree. This is the single reason the incident had a good ending, and
  it is a property of the design, not luck.
- **The transcript is preserved** — in `webui.db` and in the 17MB off-pod tarball
  taken before the invasive repair attempts.

### The episodic-overwrite hazard, and why it did not fire here

An earlier draft of this report asserted that the truncation window permanently
destroyed episodic memory. **That claim was wrong and is retracted.** It is
recorded rather than deleted, because the mechanism is real and came within one
design decision of being correct.

**The mechanism — established, from code.** `turn_index = len(messages) + 1`
(`main.py:1202`) feeds the ChromaDB document id `f"{conv_id}::{turn_index}"`
(`retrieval.py:139-144`), written with `upsert` (`retrieval.py:166`). With the
chain pinned at `msgs=3 → 5 → 7`, `turn_index` took the values 4, 6, 8. Had
those writes landed in the affected conversation's namespace, each would have
destroyed a genuine early exchange.

**Why they did not — established, by measurement.** They landed somewhere else.
A new root carries a new first user message, and `conv_id` on the fallback path
is `sha256(system|||first_user[:512])[:16]` (`memory.py:87-130`), so the
truncated branch resolved to `<scratch-conv>` — visible on every request in
the logs — not to `<user-conv>`. The overwrites consumed rows in a scratch
namespace holding 5 episodic entries.

The affected conversation's store was inspected directly via its export bundle:

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Episodic row count | 98, against ~104 exchanges in a 208-message chain |
| `turn_index` range | 2 → ~200, evenly spaced, no pinning |
| Content at turns 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 | The opening of the conversation, in sequence, verbatim |

Turns 4, 6 and 8 — the three the mechanism would have destroyed — hold their
original content. A pinned store looks like the phantom conversation
`<phantom-conv>`: 105 facts and **one** episodic row. This one does not.

**The inversion worth recording.** `conv_id` derivation from message content is
treated throughout `FRONTEND_SPEC.md` §4 as a defect to be eliminated in favour
of a stable client-supplied `X-Conversation-Id`. It is a defect — it orphaned a
conversation's memory in v3.0.1. It is also the sole reason this incident cost
no user data: a corrupted branch produced a different fingerprint and was
therefore quarantined into its own namespace automatically. **Under the stable
identifier the spec mandates, these writes would have landed on the live
conversation and the retracted claim above would have been correct.** Fixing the
conv_id defect without first fixing the episodic-identity defect (C2) would
convert this near-miss into the real thing. The ordering is not optional.

**What was actually destroyed:** roughly 104 exchanges of the phantom
conversation `<phantom-conv>`, which is background task traffic and not user
content, plus a handful of rows in the `<scratch-conv>` scratch namespace.
Neither is recoverable and neither matters.

**A second, ongoing capability reduction.** Production runs with
`COMPACTOR_MAX_RETAINED_IMAGES=0` on a **vision** model. That is stronger than
the "drop old images" it reads like: `MAX_RETAINED_IMAGES` is falsy at 0, so
`keep` at `main.py:580` is the empty set and **every image is stripped,
including the one the user just uploaded.** She can attach a picture, see it in
the composer, and the model will never receive it — with no error and no
affordance telling her. That is the intended mitigation, but its silence is a
second instance of the incident's own pattern, and `FRONTEND_SPEC.md` §8 item 4
requires the missing indicator.

It was also, until `941d339`, recorded only in `ROADMAP.md` prose — no committed
env file carried it, so a pod rebuild would have restored the default of `1` and
silently re-enabled the behaviour that headed the incident chain. Corrected in
`runpod.env.template`; the `Dockerfile` and `.env.example` model defaults were
found to have the same divergence and were corrected with it.

## 4. Root cause

### 4.1 Trigger — corrected

The first-pass account, and `b2411b1`'s commit body, state: *"the chain began
with our own image-token undercount in v3.0.4."* **That is wrong on three
counts, and the correction makes the finding larger, not smaller.**

**Wrong release.** `1536` shipped in **v3.0.2** (`1ff711f`, 22:23:44), 23 minutes
before v3.0.4. `97a39ae`'s diff does not contain `IMAGE_TOKEN_ESTIMATE`. Verified
tag by tag (§3.2).

**Wrong direction.** 768 undercounts *worse* than 1536. If the undercount alone
were sufficient, v3.0 and v3.0.1 were more exposed, not less. And v3.0.4
*reduced* per-request image cost twice over — retention moved to every request,
default cut from 3 to 1. On the account's own mechanism, v3.0.4 moved the system
away from overflow.

**Wrong shape.** The constant shipped 2026-06-08 and survived rc5–rc8, two
adversarial audit rounds, and rc7's 18-finding promotion review — during which
the very line was edited. It was wrong for **2.5 months**. Calling it a one-night
slip compresses a long-standing design property into a closed, already-patched
mistake, which is the flattering version.

**Insufficient as stated.** A vision-token undercount cannot produce vision-token
overflow on a text-only backend, and the repo shows the served model changed at
least twice inside the window, recorded nowhere but commit prose:

| Commit | Time | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `f4a03c4` | 21:45 | vLLM returning `"...is not a multimodal model"` — **text-only backend** |
| `e73fd02` | 22:41 | *"the user HAS been swapping models"* |
| `97a39ae` | 22:46 | *"the operator rolled the model back and told the tester to stop uploading pictures"* |
| `4704ef4` | 23:12 | *"Production (**vision model**, long conversation)"* — **multimodal backend** |

**Corrected trigger statement (established):**

> An unmeasured flat per-image token constant — 768 from 2026-06-08, 1536 from
> v3.0.2, 4096 today, against a true Mistral3 tiling cost of 4,000–8,000 —
> caused `count_tokens` (`main.py:283-303`, which tokenizes text only and adds
> the constant per image part) to understate request size by 3–5×.
> `_enforce_hard_budget` (`main.py:664-777`) therefore **deleted genuine
> conversation to make room for an image it had mispriced, and then certified
> success.** The undercount was made live by an undocumented mid-incident swap
> to a vision backend. The production instance is recorded in `4704ef4`'s body:
> 196 turns shed, 28,651 tokens measured, 35,510 counted by vLLM — a 6,859-token
> gap.

**And it was known when it was deferred.** `97a39ae`'s own commit body, written
22:46:36, says: *"A photo tiles to thousands of real tokens (far more than
`COMPACTOR_IMAGE_TOKENS` estimates), so three retained images can consume a third
of a 32K window."* The engineer stated the undercount as fact in the same commit
in which he declined to change the number. It was recognised and deferred for 26
minutes and one more production 400. The correct five-minute patch at 22:46 was
`IMAGE_TOKEN_ESTIMATE = 8192` — entailed by the sentence he had just typed, and
the number `4704ef4` later recommends to operators while shipping 4096 in code.

**Three concurrent 400 sources, not one.** The account collapses the window into
a single context-length defect. The repo shows three independent, simultaneously
live defects each producing a vLLM 400, any of which could have interrupted a
request:

- modality 400 (`"is not a multimodal model"`) — fixed v3.0.1
- **alternation 400** (`user,user` after compaction hoists image turns) —
  described in `1ff711f` as *"a deterministic 400 on any long image
  conversation"* — fixed v3.0.2
- context-length 400 — mitigated v3.0.5

`1ff711f` additionally records a production **engine stall**: *"UNBOUNDED IMAGE
ACCUMULATION (suspected cause of the 2026-08-24 stall) … the failure mode is a
hang, not a clean error."* The stall is absent from the first-pass narrative
entirely, and a hang is precisely the condition that produces user-initiated
regenerate or edit.

### 4.2 Transition — **NOT ESTABLISHED. Do not cite the 400-to-root mechanism.**

The first-pass account states: *"On each 400, OpenWebUI created the failed turn
as a NEW ROOT in its message tree and moved currentId to it."* **The code refutes
this on the path that matters.**

`chat_completions` has two structurally different 4xx behaviours, **unchanged
between `v3.0` and HEAD**:

- **Streaming** (`main.py:1451-1476`, verified): a `status_code >= 400` is caught
  *inside the generator*, after `StreamingResponse` has already committed
  **HTTP 200**. The compactor emits `_vllm_unreachable_stream_chunks(...,
  message=REQUEST_REJECTED_MESSAGE)` — a well-formed SSE pair ending
  `finish_reason: "stop"` — then `data: [DONE]`. To OpenWebUI this is an
  ordinary successful completion whose assistant text happens to read *"That
  request couldn't be processed…"*.
- **Non-streaming** (`main.py:1571-1573`, verified): the 400 is relayed verbatim.
  This is the path OpenWebUI's background title/tag tasks take.

**OpenWebUI's chat path is streaming. At v3.0.4 the compactor could not hand the
chat UI an error to fork on.**

Load-bearing inferences that were being presented as fact, named:

| # | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | 400s caused new roots | **Refuted** on the streaming path; unestablished on any path |
| I2 | The 5 roots were created in the ~45-min damage window | Roots measured ~10h later; no time series exists |
| I3 | 208 was the pre-incident length | No expected count was ever recorded anywhere |
| I4 | "Damage stopped when the 400s stopped" | Uninstrumented; the 400s and the pod restarts stopped together |
| I5 | An open browser tab reverted the repairs | Never observed directly; equally consistent with a second restore or blob/table divergence |
| I6 | OpenWebUI 0.11 reads `chat_message`, not `chat.chat` | Asserted; not verifiable in this repo — and it is the whole basis of detection failure §5.4 |
| I7 | `chat_message.id` is `{chat_id}-{uuid}` vs bare-uuid `parent_id` | Asserted; not verifiable here — and now written into `FRONTEND_SPEC.md §11.2` as a design requirement |

**Correct statement of the boundary:**

> The compactor owns the undercount, the mis-aimed shedding, and the request vLLM
> rejected. vLLM owns the 400. **Whatever produced `roots=5` is OpenWebUI-internal
> and was never instrumented; the mechanism is now unrecoverable.** Candidates,
> none excluded: user-initiated first-message edit or regenerate during the engine
> stall (the parentless-node path `FRONTEND_SPEC.md:658-660` names as *"one
> plausible source of the five roots"*); client retry across the **four to five
> pod restarts** the release cadence itself produced inside the damage window;
> blob/table divergence under a MooseFS `disk I/O error`.

That last candidate makes the release cadence *directly* causal rather than
merely attention-consuming, and it is the possibility the first-pass analysis
never considered.

One divergence the streaming path definitely creates: `vllm_failed = True`
suppresses the memory tail (`main.py:1506`), so a user turn on a failed request
lands in OpenWebUI's transcript but is **never indexed by the compactor**.
Transcript and memory disagree on precisely the failed turns.

### 4.3 Amplifiers — what turned a bad request into invisible data

| # | Amplifier | Why it amplifies |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | `currentId` is a bare mutable field with **no invariant and no validation**. Nothing requires it to descend from a root, be the deepest chain, or belong to this conversation at all. | Damage is one O(1) write. Recovery requires reconstructing an intent the system never recorded. |
| A2 | Context sent = `walk(parentId, currentId)`. **The read path derives the most consequential decision in the product from the same unvalidated pointer the write path corrupted.** | One corrupted field produces both H1 and H2. |
| A3 | **No count is ever disclosed or compared.** The compactor logs `msgs=`; nothing anywhere holds an expected value. | `241` and `7` sat in two logs two seconds apart and nothing subtracted them. |
| A4 | Dual-write (blob + table), whole-conversation, client-authoritative on write. | Made repair non-durable and lets the two stores diverge silently. |
| A5 | Streaming 4xx → HTTP 200 + synthetic assistant turn (`main.py:1451-1476`). | Honest to a human, **invisible to every machine**. No client, monitor or test can distinguish "model replied" from "request rejected." |
| A6 | Memory is `conv_id`-scoped, so it **survived completely intact**. | Double-edged: every memory-side check came back green all night and reinforced "healthy" — and it is the only reason the migration had anything to migrate. |

### 4.4 Latent conditions

- **L1 — a guess where an invariant is required, for 2.5 months** (§4.1).
- **L2 — the self-healing corrector cannot converge past its cap, and a passing
  test defends it.** `_note_backend_rejection` (`main.py:216`, verified) computes
  `overshoot = actual - HARD_INPUT_LIMIT` — against the **module constant**, not
  against the limit actually applied, which is `effective_limit - _BUDGET_MARGIN`
  (`main.py:695-699`, `1418-1425`). The binding constraint, however, is the cap:
  `new_margin = min(overshoot + 512, MAX_MODEL_LEN // 4)` clamps at 8192, so
  **any true-minus-estimated gap `U ≥ 8192` never converges**, and correcting the
  overshoot arithmetic alone does not change that. At the current default of 4096
  with retention 1, realistic `U` is ~4–7k and the production case (`U = 6,859`)
  does converge — so this is a live latent defect, not the incident's cause. The
  properties that fire on *every* calibration event are worse: `_BUDGET_MARGIN`
  is **process-global** (one image-heavy conversation shrinks the usable window
  for every conversation, by up to 8192 tokens) and **never reset** (only a pod
  restart recovers, and a restart is the thing most likely to happen next).
  `test_modality.py:261-268` asserts *"calibration is monotonic — a smaller
  report never loosens it"* and passes; `4704ef4`'s own body concedes *"the test
  window is 1000 tokens, so the cap dominates there."* **The test cannot exercise
  the failing regime by construction, and currently constitutes a written
  specification of the wrong behaviour.**
- **L3 — position is defined as the corrupted quantity.** `turn_index =
  len(messages) + 1` (`main.py:1202`). **Consequence: under truncation the
  compactor degrades silently by construction** — it cannot detect a short
  context, because its notion of position *is* the short length. Production
  proof: phantom conversation `<phantom-conv>` holds 105 facts and exactly
  **one** indexed episodic exchange, because every `msgs=1` task call yields
  `turn_index = 2` and upserts the same `conv::2`; a comparable real conversation
  holds 105 and 98.
- **L4 — no invariant enforcement exists for conversation structure anywhere.**
  Neither store carries a one-root index or a leaf foreign key.
- **L5 — SQLite and ChromaDB on MooseFS.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:137-155` Decision 4,
  *"Decided (2026-08-10, forced by a production incident)"*, designates a
  Postgres + pgvector sidecar as the state home. It is not built. This is what
  made *"a database restore had overwritten the repair"* a live event during
  recovery. **Correction to the account:** the repo documents **one** such
  incident (2026-08-10), not two in two weeks.
- **L6 — noise floor.** 119 conversations of which ~30 are real; the rest are
  `__selftest_oneshot_*` (one per boot, unbounded) and `itest-*` artifacts.
  `CLONE_CONV_ID_HERE` exists as a real conversation because a placeholder from a
  command was pasted unsubstituted and the "sanitizer" accepted it. None of these
  caused the incident; all of them degraded every forensic query run during it.
- **L7 — the only correctness oracle was a non-technical user's felt sense.** No
  staging, no end-to-end assertion of what the model was actually sent, one
  production user.

### 4.5 Five whys — where the chain actually bottoms out

1. The user lost her conversation and the assistant answered from 7 of 241
   messages. → Because `currentId` named a node on an 8-message subtree with its
   own root, and both the render path and the send path derive everything from
   `currentId`.
2. Why could it name that node? → Because nothing relates the leaf pointer to the
   tree. It is a string in a JSON blob and a column in a table, written by
   whoever writes last.
3. Why is it unconstrained? → Because conversation state is a **document** —
   dual-written, mutated by whole-object writes from a client holding the
   authoritative copy in memory. Document storage with client-side authority
   cannot express a cross-record invariant; there is no place to put "exactly one
   root."
4. Why is state stored that way? → Because the front end was adopted, not
   designed, and the project's ownership boundary stops at the compactor.
5. Why was the boundary drawn there? → Because the continuity the product
   promises was implemented *entirely* in the layer the team owns — facts, RAG,
   L1/L2/L3, personas, all `conv_id`-scoped, all audited, all intact through the
   incident — on the assumption that the transcript layer was a solved substrate.

**The defensible floor is not "we adopted OpenWebUI."** A bespoke client with the
same document-write model fails identically; ownership is not the property that
matters, **enforcement** is. The floor is:

> **The system had a rich model of what it remembered and no model at all of what
> it was told. Every layer knew *content*; no layer knew *completeness*.**

One property explains all four branches simultaneously. **Trigger:** a guess
stands where an invariant is required, inside the component whose own docstring
calls it the last line of defense. **Amplifier:** one unvalidated pointer decides
both what is shown and what is sent, because no layer holds an independent
statement of what should be sent. **Latency:** position is defined as the client's
array length, so truncation is undetectable by construction. **Detection:** with
no definition of correct, every check is a descriptive statistic, and adjacent
descriptive statistics substitute for each other freely.

Two components each knew the conversation's length. `241` and `7` were logged two
seconds apart. Nothing in the system was responsible for subtracting them.

## 5. Why detection and diagnosis took as long as they did

The duration figure has been corrected downward (§3.1). The analysis below stands
on the **quality** of the errors, not their length.

### 5.1 The structural fact

Damage is a write: O(1). Diagnosis is a search over hypotheses in a system that
**has no ground truth about its own state** — no expected message count, no
recorded send-intent, no invariant, no cross-check. In such a system every
measurement is *descriptive* and none is *normative*, so "healthy" cannot be
falsified, because "healthy" has no definition. Everything below follows.

### 5.2 The three "healthy" declarations

The measured tuple was `total=241 · deepest=208 · roots=5 · currentId=d1226204 ·
chain_from_current=8`. The engineer verified `deepest=208` and stopped. Five
reasons, four of them properties of the *system*:

1. **The check was the user's sentence transcribed.** She said "I lost 208
   messages." The literal verification of *did we lose 208 messages* is *do 208
   messages exist in a chain*, and that has a true answer: yes. Absence and
   misdirection have identical phenomenology and non-identical measurements. The
   user cannot tell you which she is experiencing; only the pointer can.
2. **`deepest` is a property of the data; `chain_from_current` is a property of
   the data plus one pointer** — a pointer that holds no content, is displayed
   nowhere, and is not part of "the conversation" in anyone's mental model.
3. **The check re-read its own premise.** The first repair *found the longest
   chain (208) and set `currentId` to its tip*. Verifying `deepest=208`
   afterwards measures the repair's **input**, not its **effect**. All three
   declarations have this shape.
4. **The proxy is exactly correct on 100% of healthy inputs.** `deepest ≥
   chain_from_current` always, with equality in every non-pathological
   conversation. A proxy that is right on every input you have ever seen and
   wrong only on the defective one is the hardest possible proxy to distrust.
5. **The system supplied no name for the right property.** OpenWebUI *stores* a
   tree and *presents* a list. Every UI surface, export and mental model is
   linear. You cannot refuse to substitute a wrong property for the right one if
   the right one has no name in your head.

**General form, for the record:** *verifying a repair against a quantity the
repair itself set, in a state space where the failing property has no
representation in the interface.*

At least one of these declarations was **communicated outward**, after which the
user continued using a conversation that was silently sending 7 of 241 messages.
The error is not only measuring the wrong thing; it is reporting confidence that
had not been earned, to someone who then acted on it. That the underlying store
turned out to be intact (§2) does not reduce the error — the confidence was
unearned at the time it was given, and it was given three times.

### 5.3 The composite-key false positive

`chat_message`'s primary key is `{chat_id}-{uuid}`; `parent_id` holds a bare
`uuid`. Two fields naming the same entities in **different key spaces with no
schema constraint linking them**. Any join written from inspection rather than
from the schema is wrong, and its failure mode is *100% false positives* — it
reports total orphaning, which is maximally alarming and therefore maximally
attention-consuming. **A false alarm costs more investigative time than silence,
because it is actionable.**

### 5.4 Verifying against the wrong store

OpenWebUI 0.11 is understood to read from the normalized `chat_message` table
(**inferred, I6**); verification ran against the `chat.chat` JSON blob. This is
§5.2's error one level down — an adjacent *artifact* rather than an adjacent
*property* — and it is available only because the system maintains two writable
representations of one truth with no constraint between them, on a filesystem
that can lose one of them.

### 5.5 The branch-closing move

Attributing the scroll-bouncing to `open-webui#23990` and reproducing it in two
browsers is the most dangerous class of diagnostic move, because it **closes a
branch**: an external attribution to a real, documented upstream bug is often
true and never sufficient. Reproduction in two browsers reads as confirmation; it
confirms only that the symptom is client-side. The failure was not undertaking
that investigation — it was not time-boxing it against the primary hypothesis.

### 5.6 The tooling had the same failure mode as the product

Five to six subagent/workflow runs died on 529 Overloaded. **An empty workflow
result is byte-identical to a clean bill of health.** To the engineer's credit,
this lesson was drawn in real time, not retrofitted: `1ff711f` records *"the
review workflow 529'd twice"* and `e73fd02` records *"six agents, zero
completions; the empty result looked like a clean bill and was not one."* The
consequence stands regardless: three of the five patches shipped with no
independent review. **The night ran on a stack in which both the product and the
diagnostic tooling return silence for failure.**

### 5.7 Non-durable repair as an anti-signal

Twice, a correct repair was reverted (**I5** — the open-tab mechanism is
inferred, not observed). This produces the worst available epistemic state:
**correct action generating evidence of incorrect diagnosis.** A diagnostic loop
in which the fix can be silently undone by an unidentified actor does not
terminate.

**The decision point that was missed** is identifiable and it is the second
reverted repair. At that moment the system had demonstrated two things: the
target state was not durably writable by the repairer, and the authoritative
writer was outside the repairer's control. Repair as a strategy requires a write
that sticks. A third attempt differed from the first two in no relevant respect.

**General rule, stated so it is usable:** *repair when the damaged thing is the
thing of value **and** you own the only writer. Fail either test and rebuild.*
Both tests failed here — the transcript was not the value, and the browser owned
the write.

### 5.8 How the loop actually broke

It broke on a **semantic** observation, not a structural one: the user asked the
assistant what it remembered, and the answer was inconsistent with what the
memory store demonstrably contained. That is the only measurement in the whole
incident taken against an **expectation held outside the system**.

**The cheapest reliable oracle available all night was the user's own
expectation, and it was consulted last.**

## 6. What went right

- **Refusal to hot-patch production — the best decision in the record.** Five
  immutable tags, each with a build, each rollback-addressable. On a night with a
  demonstrably non-zero error rate, this is what bounded the blast radius and
  made "which version was running when?" answerable at all. The timeline in §3 is
  only reconstructible because of it. Partly undercut by the changelog gap (D14):
  the tags are precise, their contents are undocumented.
- **`conv_id`-scoped memory outside the client.** The single design decision that
  determined this incident had a good ending. 105 facts, 98 episodic exchanges,
  5 L1 chunks and the persona survived a completely corrupted client tree because
  they were never in it.
- **The 17MB off-pod tarball — correct, and correctly timed.** Taken *before* the
  invasive attempts, and taken **off-pod**, which is the property that mattered;
  `OPERATIONS.md` is explicit that the on-volume backup daemon does not protect
  against volume loss. This is what made the eventual transcript abandonment
  acceptable rather than final.
- **The clone to the owner's account — better than it was credited.** It moved
  experimentation off a real user's data under pressure, and it **exercised the
  export path that later resolved the incident**. The tool built for debugging
  was the tool for recovery, and it was in hand within the first hours.
- **Tests in every patch commit.** `test_modality.py` grows in all five. Under
  time pressure this is the first discipline usually dropped, and it was not.
- **Honest degradation as a consistent design value.** v3.0.1's placeholder
  *tells* the model an image existed and cannot be seen rather than silently
  vanishing it. Both reactive backstops — modality flip, budget tightening —
  heal on the next message instead of leaving a conversation poisoned. The irony
  worth naming: a codebase this committed to not failing silently produced a
  ten-hour silent-truncation incident, because the honesty was designed into the
  **backend** boundary and the failure was at the **client** boundary, which
  nobody owned.
- **`FRONTEND_SPEC.md` §4.1's prohibition list.** *"The following must not be
  used as an integrity check"* with the wrong checks enumerated **by name**
  (`max_depth`, `count(messages) == expected`, `chain_from_current ≥ threshold`),
  plus a standing test case with concrete numbers (241 messages, 5 roots, current
  leaf at depth 8 — the check must fail). This is the correct way to write down a
  measurement lesson, and far better than a stated principle would have been.
  It is also, per D16, not yet applied consistently within its own document.
- **The superseded ROADMAP note that keeps the wrong original analysis unedited
  and says so** (*"it was wrong in an instructive way"*). Rare practice, correct,
  keep it. The documented prior position was **"Default position: don't"** on
  front-end replacement, which is real evidence the eventual conclusion was not
  pre-loaded.

**Reasonable in the moment though wrong in hindsight:** the first repair (longest
chain → `currentId`) was defensible on the information available. The error was
not the action but believing it without a check that could have refuted it. Note
the consistency of the eventual conclusion — §4.1 now forbids exactly that
automatic pointer move.

Fixes the episodic id and the RAG cutoff; the L1 rollup gate (D22) stays client-derived and is deferred to a real `seq` in the transcript store. **Ordering constraint: this must land before any change that makes `conv_id` stable across a corrupted branch** — §2 establishes that content-derived `conv_id` is the only thing that quarantined the bad writes, so removing that property first converts a near-miss into real data loss. | D1, §2 | **PLANNED** |

### Explicitly not adopted

- **Replacing OpenWebUI is not a corrective action for this incident.** The
  documented prior position was *"Default position: don't"*, which defeats the
  charge that the conclusion was pre-loaded. But `FRONTEND_SPEC.md` was drafted
  under the write-path diagnosis (failed turn → new root), which §4.2 shows is
  unestablished, and then revised to absorb the read-path finding that replaced
  it — **the conclusion survived the wholesale replacement of the evidence that
  produced it.** Its stated justification (E8, *"structurally immune to that
  entire class of failure"*) was retracted the same day. The replacement may
  still be the right call for roadmap, ownership and compactor-native reasons;
  it should not cite 2026-08-24 as its cause. What this incident supports is
  C3, C5 and C11 — the invariant and the disclosure — and those are extractable
  and can be applied to the existing schema.
- **Fixing the token estimate is not the corrective action either.** It was
  corrected twice in one night and was wrong both times (768 → 1536 → 4096,
  against a true 4,000–8,000, with `4704ef4` itself telling operators to set
  8192). A number cannot be the control. The pointer defect fires on *any* failed
  request, and requests fail for OOM, pod restart, MooseFS I/O error, model
  reload and network — `_enforce_hard_budget`'s own docstring records a
  2026-08-13 incident where the same guard failed for entirely non-image reasons.
  The estimate is still worth setting correctly (C1); it is a mitigation.
- **Better repair tooling and more backups.** Repair ran four times and was
  reverted twice; the data was never lost, so backups address a harm that did not
  occur and the 17MB tarball was never needed. Resolution came from abandoning
  repair. The tooling that mattered — portability — already existed and worked.

## 9. Lessons

Three. Each is stated so it can be checked against a future decision.

### 1. A measurement is only evidence if it can come out wrong.

`deepest=208` was true, was checked three times, and was compatible with total
user-visible loss. It could not have refuted the symptom. The failure mode is
specific and repeatable: *verifying a repair against a quantity the repair itself
set, in a state space where the failing property has no representation in the
interface.* The counter-practice is equally specific — before fixing anything,
write the user's report as a predicate that a healthy system passes and a broken
one fails, then state its falsifier. "A 208-message chain exists" has no
falsifier. "The next request carries 241 messages" is refuted in one turn — and
was already being measured, because the compactor logs `msgs=` on every single
request. **The instrument existed and printed the answer continuously for ten
hours. Nobody had named the quantity it reports as the thing that defines
health.**

### 2. Every layer knew content; no layer knew completeness.

`241` and `7` were logged two seconds apart and nothing in the system was
responsible for subtracting them. That is the whole incident. A system that
tracks what it remembers, and does not track what it was told, will degrade
silently under any upstream truncation — and cannot detect the degradation,
because `turn_index = len(messages) + 1` defines its own yardstick as the thing
being measured. The corrective is not a better estimate or a better client; it is
that **some layer must hold an independent statement of what should have arrived,
and compare.** C3 before C11: the comparison is cheaper, more general, and the
only control that fires on the harm that had no symptom.

### 3. A fast loop converges on symptoms, because a disappearing symptom looks like a fix.

Five patches in 87 minutes, each individually justified and locally correct, all
five local repairs to one systemic fact: *the compactor prices images with a
constant.* The intervals shrink toward the moment of maximum consequence — 38m →
17m → **5m 35s** → 26m — and the 5m 35s window is v3.0.4, the release whose own
commit body states the undercount as known fact and does not change it. Speed did
not cause bad decisions; it caused **narrow** ones, because every patch was
validated by its symptom disappearing, which is adjacent-property substitution at
the release level. Nothing in the loop forced a step up from patch to model.

The counter-evidence must be kept honest: the same cadence produced v3.0.3 within
17 minutes, repairing a bug v3.0.1 had introduced 56 minutes earlier that
orphaned **memory** — the one asset that survived the night intact and made the
recovery possible. A slower line leaves memory-orphaning in production for days.
**Cadence is not the root cause. The absent step-back is.** Two rules that keep
the speed and remove the narrowness: *one deploy, one observation, one decision*
— do not cut tag N+1 until tag N has produced a real signal (the 1536 estimate
dies in sixty seconds under that rule: one real photo, one `len(tok.encode(...))`);
and *a self-inflicted fix is a stop signal* — v3.0.3 fixing v3.0.1 was the night's
error rate becoming measurably non-zero, and the correct response to that datum
is to stop cutting and go verify.
